Customer not paying? What to do in Malaysia

Customer not paying your invoice? 5 steps in order: reminders to copy, a letter of demand and small claims for RM 5,000 or less, plus what to keep as proof.

Invois team, EONGERRA TECH

6 min read · Last updated 6 November 2026

In short
  • The steps, in order: check your invoice, remind politely, remind again and call, send a letter of demand, then claim.
  • Small claims: for RM 5,000 or less, you file Form 198 at the Magistrates' Court yourself, with no lawyer. Only an individual can claim: a Sdn Bhd cannot, but you can claim against one.
  • Keep as proof: the quotation, the invoice, the chats where the customer agreed, photos or files of the finished work, and every reminder.
  • Time limit: in Peninsular Malaysia, normally 6 years from the date payment was due.

What to do when a customer does not pay: 5 steps

  1. Check your side first. Make sure the customer has the invoice, with the right amount and a due date that has passed.
  2. Send a polite reminder the day after the due date, with the invoice number, the amount, the due date and the invoice attached again.
  3. Send a second reminder and call about a week later. Ask for a date, offer to split the amount if money is tight, and hold off new work for this customer.
  4. Send a letter of demand if you still have no payment or no clear date: the amount, a deadline and what you will do next.
  5. Make a claim once the deadline passes. For RM 5,000 or less, use small claims yourself; for more, ask a lawyer about the normal court process.

Do not shame the customer in public or contact their family or employer.

What a letter of demand says

There is no set format, but it should be clear to someone who has not seen your chats.

FromYour name, business name, SSM number if registered, phone and address
ToThe customer's full name and address
Date22 December 2026
AboutInvoice INV-2611-021, 16 November 2026: café logo and menu design
Amount owedRM 1,800.00 invoiced, RM 540.00 paid, RM 1,260.00 outstanding since 30 November 2026
DeadlinePay in full within 14 days, by 5 January 2027
How to payYour bank, account name and account number
If unpaidYou will file a claim at the Magistrates' Court without further notice
SignedYour signature and name

Send it by WhatsApp or email and by registered post, and keep the proof of posting: the court papers can later go to the same address.

Small claims: RM 5,000 or less, no lawyer

Small claims is for claims of up to RM 5,000 made by an individual. If you work under your own name or a sole proprietorship, you normally claim in your own name; a Sdn Bhd cannot claim this way.

If your business should be registered with SSM and is not, you may not be able to enforce the contract: see Do I need to register with SSM?

  1. Get Form 198 at the Magistrates' Court registry, or download it from the Selangor court portal.
  2. Pick the court. Normally, file at the court for the area where the customer lives or does business, or where the claim arose, such as where the job was done.
  3. File it. Fill in your full name and IC number, the amount and what it is for, and sign it yourself. File 4 copies at the registry and pay the filing fee, listed as RM 20.00. The registry stamps them, adds the hearing date and gives you 1 back.
  4. Serve it. Give a stamped copy to the customer by hand, or send it by prepaid registered post.
  5. File an affidavit of service: a statement of how and when you served it, sworn before a Commissioner for Oaths (Pesuruhjaya Sumpah). The Selangor court portal has the form.
  6. Wait for the defence. The customer has 14 days after being served to file a defence. If they do not, the court may give judgment for you on the hearing date.
  7. Go to the hearing with your witnesses and documents, including the letter of demand and the proof of posting. Neither side may bring a lawyer, but you can ask one for advice first.
  8. Settle or get judgment. The magistrate may help you settle. If you win, the court can also order the customer to pay up to RM 100 of your costs.
  9. If they still do not pay, ask the court to call them in to explain why (a notice to show cause, Form 208). The court can then order their goods seized and sold, or let them pay in instalments, among other orders.

This is general information, not legal advice.

How long you have to claim

In Peninsular Malaysia, a claim for money owed under an agreement must normally be filed within 6 years from the date payment was due.

  • If the customer makes a part payment, or admits the debt in writing and signs it, the 6 years start again from that date.
  • Sabah and Sarawak have their own limitation laws.

If you decide the money is lost, it may be claimable as a bad debt: see business expenses on Form B.

FAQ

Can I add interest for late payment?

Normally only at a rate the customer agreed to before the job, usually in your quotation terms. For the wording, see quotation terms and conditions.

The customer says the work is not done properly. What now?

Ask what is wrong in writing and fix what is fair. If you cannot agree, a magistrate can decide a disputed small claim after hearing both sides.

Do I need a lawyer to send a letter of demand?

No. You can write and sign it yourself. Some customers take a lawyer's letter more seriously, but it costs a fee.

Can I use small claims against a company?

Yes. Use the company's full name, with Sdn Bhd, as it appears on its documents.

The customer stopped replying or blocked me. What now?

Send the letter of demand by registered post too. A small claim can be served the same way, so you do not need them to reply.

Sources

  • Small claims: Rules of Court 2012, Order 93, rules 1 to 8 and 13 to 16.
  • Which court: Subordinate Courts Act 1948, section 76(2) and Third Schedule, paragraph 2(1).
  • Form 198, the RM 20.00 fee, the hearing date and the affidavit of service: Mahkamah Negeri Selangor, Prosedur Tuntutan Kecil and its forms (Form 198, Afidavit Penyampaian).
  • Time limit: Limitation Act 1953, sections 1(2), 6(1)(a), 26(2) and 27(1).
