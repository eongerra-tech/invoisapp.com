Customer not paying? What to do in Malaysia

Customer not paying your invoice? 5 steps in order: reminders to copy, a letter of demand and small claims for RM 5,000 or less, plus what to keep as proof.

Invois team, EONGERRA TECH

9 min read · Last updated 29 October 2026

In short
  • The steps, in order: check your invoice, a polite reminder the day after the due date, a second reminder and a call about a week later, a letter of demand, then a claim.
  • Small claims: for RM 5,000 or less, you file Form 198 at the Magistrates' Court yourself, with no lawyer. Only an individual can claim: a Sdn Bhd cannot, but you can claim against one.
  • Keep as proof: the quotation, the invoice, the chats where the customer agreed, photos or files of the finished work, and every reminder.
  • Time limit: in Peninsular Malaysia, normally 6 years from the date payment was due.

Start with the mildest step and move to the next one only when the customer stays silent or keeps putting you off. Keep a written record of every step, and keep it polite and private.

What to do when a customer does not pay: 5 steps

1. Check your side first

Make sure the customer has the invoice, with the right amount and a due date, and that the due date has passed. A company may also need the invoice sent to its accounts team, with its PO number.

If the customer has complained about the work, that is a dispute, not a late payment. Settle the complaint first, and put what you agree in writing.

2. Send a polite reminder

The day after the due date, send a short message with the invoice number, the amount, the due date and the invoice attached again. Ask when they can pay.

3. Send a second reminder and call

About a week later, remind them again and phone. Ask for a date, and offer to split the amount into 2 or 3 payments if money is tight. Hold off new work for this customer until the invoice is paid.

4. Send a letter of demand

If 2 reminders and a call get no payment or no clear date, send a letter of demand: a formal letter with the amount owed, a deadline and what you will do next. You can write and sign it yourself. See Letter of demand.

5. Make a claim

If the deadline passes, file a claim. For RM 5,000 or less, the small claims procedure lets you do it yourself (Small claims). For more than that, ask a lawyer about the normal court process.

First reminder (1 December): "Hi Encik Faizal, hope you're well. A reminder that the balance of RM 1,260.00 on invoice INV-2611-021, for the café logo and menu, was due on 30 November. I've attached the invoice again. Could you let me know when you can transfer it? Thank you."

Second reminder (8 December): "Hi Encik Faizal, following up on the café logo and menu: the balance of RM 1,260.00 is now 8 days overdue. Could you transfer it by 12 December, or tell me a date that works? If it helps, you can pay it in 2 parts of RM 630.00."

Last message (15 December): "Hi Encik Faizal, I still haven't received the RM 1,260.00 for the café logo and menu, due on 30 November. If it isn't paid by 21 December, I will send a letter of demand, and then file a small claim. I would much rather settle it with you directly."

Keep every message, and the customer's replies: if you make a claim, they are your evidence. Do not shame the customer in public or contact their family or employer.

What a letter of demand says

A letter of demand is your last written request before you claim. It is not a court form, so there is no set format, but it should be clear enough for someone who has not seen your chats.

FromYour name, business name, SSM number if registered, phone and address
ToThe customer's full name and address
Date22 December 2026
AboutInvoice INV-2611-021, 16 November 2026: café logo and menu design
Amount owedRM 1,800.00 invoiced, RM 540.00 paid, RM 1,260.00 outstanding since 30 November 2026
DeadlinePay in full within 14 days, by 5 January 2027
How to payYour bank, account name and account number
If unpaidYou will file a claim at the Magistrates' Court without further notice
SignedYour signature and name

Send it by WhatsApp or email, and also by registered post to the customer's address, and keep the proof of posting. If you claim later, you will need that address: the claim form can be served by prepaid registered post to the customer's last known address. A lawyer can send the letter for you, for a fee.

Small claims: RM 5,000 or less, no lawyer

The small claims procedure at the Magistrates' Court is for claims of up to RM 5,000, made by an individual. If you work under your own name or a sole proprietorship, you normally claim in your own name. A company cannot claim this way, but you can claim against a company.

  • Filing: fill in Form 198 with the amount and what it is for, sign it yourself, and file 4 copies at the Magistrates' Court registry. The Judiciary lists the filing fee as RM 20.00. You can also start it online on the Judiciary's e-filing portal and pay at the counter.
  • Serving: you give the sealed claim to the customer, by hand or by prepaid registered post, then file an affidavit of service.
  • Defence: the customer has 14 days to file a defence. If they do not, the court may give judgment for you on the hearing date.
  • Hearing: no lawyers on either side, though you may ask one for advice beforehand. The magistrate reads your documents, hears both of you and may help you settle. Costs awarded are capped at RM 100.
  • If they still do not pay: you can file a notice to show cause (Form 208). The court can then order their goods seized and sold, or let them pay in instalments, among other orders.

This is general information, not legal advice. For the forms and current fees, ask the Magistrates' Court registry near you.

References: Rules of Court 2012, Order 93, rules 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 13, 14, 15 and 16; Judiciary of Malaysia, Small Claim (filing fees).

What to keep as proof

  • The quotation, or the chat where you agreed the price.
  • The invoice and its payment terms.
  • Messages where the customer approved the work or asked for changes.
  • Photos of the finished work, or the files you delivered.
  • Receipts for anything already paid, such as the deposit (How to write a receipt).
  • Every reminder, the letter of demand and the proof of posting.

How long you have to claim

In Peninsular Malaysia, a claim for money owed under an agreement must normally be filed within 6 years from the date payment was due. If the customer makes a part payment, or admits the debt in writing and signs it, the 6 years start again from that date.

Sabah and Sarawak have their own limitation laws. Do not wait that long in any case: the longer a debt sits, the harder it is to collect.

References: Limitation Act 1953, sections 1(2), 6(1)(a), 26(2) and 27(1).

Making the next one less likely

  • Take a deposit before you start, and bill larger jobs in stages.
  • Put the payment terms on the quotation, so the customer agrees to them before the work begins (Quotation terms and conditions).
  • Send the invoice as soon as the job or stage is done, with a due date.

FAQ

Can I add interest for late payment?

Normally only at a rate the customer agreed to before the job, usually in your quotation terms. Without an agreed rate, ask for the invoice amount. How to word the clause: Quotation terms and conditions.

The customer says the work is not done properly. What now?

Ask exactly what is wrong, in writing, and fix what is fair. If you agree on a lower amount, confirm it in a message and invoice the new amount. If you cannot agree, a magistrate can decide a disputed small claim after hearing both sides.

Do I need a lawyer to send a letter of demand?

No. You can write and sign it yourself. Some customers take a lawyer's letter more seriously, but it costs a fee.

What if the customer owes more than RM 5,000?

The small claims procedure does not apply, so ask a lawyer about the normal court process. The reminders and the letter of demand come first either way.

Can I use small claims against a company?

Yes. The claim must come from an individual, but the customer can be a company. Use the company's full name, with Sdn Bhd, as it appears on its documents.
