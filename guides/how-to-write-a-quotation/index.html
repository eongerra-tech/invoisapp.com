How to write a quotation in Malaysia (with examples)

What to put on a quotation in Malaysia: SSM no., SST, validity and payment terms, with worked examples from 3 different trades.

8 min read · Last updated 23 October 2026

In short
  • Put these on it: your business details, the customer and site, a number and valid-until date, the items, the total, payment terms, what is included, and a way to accept.

  • Add SST only if you are registered with JKDM.

A quotation (in Malay sebut harga, in Chinese 报价单) is a written price offer you give a customer before you start a job. It lists the work item by item, the total, how long the price is valid, and how you want to be paid. It does not ask for payment. You send an invoice once the customer accepts and the work is due.

A clear quotation wins more jobs than a bare total on WhatsApp, stops arguments later about what was included, and makes your invoice easy to write. That is true whether you fix sinks, shoot weddings, clean houses or give tuition.

A quotation comes before the work, an invoice asks for payment, and a receipt confirms you were paid. Keep the same customer details and item lines on all three. For the full comparison, with one job followed from deposit to final receipt, see quotation vs invoice vs receipt.

What to put on a quotation in Malaysia

1. Your business details

Business name, address, phone and email. If you are registered with SSM, add your registration number (the new format is 12 digits, for example 202401012345).

Companies, and sole proprietors and partnerships registered with SSM, must show this number on their business documents, and corporate customers often will not process a quotation without it.

2. Customer and site

Customer name (and company, if they are buying for one), phone, and the site address if the work happens there. For a company, add the person to contact ("Attn: Ms Tan, Facilities").

3. Title, number and dates

The word QUOTATION at the top, a quotation number you can find later (for example Q-2610-001), the date, and a valid until date. Validity of 7 to 30 days is common; 14 days is a sensible default. Without it, a customer can come back months later and ask for last year's price.

4. Itemised lines

One line per item: description, quantity, unit (unit, sqft, hour, package), unit price and amount. Split labour and materials when the customer is likely to compare. If you give something free, list it at RM 0.00 and mark it FOC: the customer sees what you gave for free instead of never knowing about it. Lines are quicker to write from a price list you set up once.

5. Totals and SST

Subtotal, discount (if any) and total, in RM with 2 decimal places (RM 1,234.50).

Only add SST if you are registered with the Royal Malaysian Customs Department (JKDM) for sales tax or service tax.

  • Registered: show SST as its own line with the rate that applies to your goods or service, and print your SST registration number.
  • Not registered: do not add SST at all.

Registration is generally required once taxable sales or services pass RM 500,000 in 12 months for most goods and services (some services, such as construction work, have a higher threshold). Check the MySST portal for yours, or see Do I need to charge SST?

6. Payment terms

Deposit (percentage or amount), when the balance is due, instalments if the job is long, and how to pay (bank name, account name and number, or DuitNow QR). For example: "30% deposit to confirm, balance on completion."

7. Scope and terms

What is included, what is not included, warranty, what happens if the job changes once you open the wall or see the site, and cancellation. Short, plain sentences work better than legal-sounding ones. There are 20 examples in Quotation terms and conditions.

8. Acceptance

A signature line, or simply: "To accept, sign and return, or reply 'OK' on WhatsApp." Keep that reply: it is your written acceptance.

Once the customer accepts, the next document is the invoice: see How to write an invoice.

3 examples from different trades

The prices below are made up to show the format. They are not a price guide. None of these businesses is SST-registered, so there is no SST line.

Plumber: kitchen sink and floor trap

2 Clear blocked floor trap 1 80.00 80.00

Total RM 275.00

Valid 14 days. Payment in full on completion. Workmanship warranty 3 months.

Wedding photographer: pre-wedding, outstation

No. Description Qty Unit price (RM) Amount (RM)

Valid 30 days; the date is only held once the deposit is paid. 30% deposit (RM 570.00) to book, balance RM 1,330.00 7 days before the shoot.

Home cleaning: move-out deep clean, 3-room condo

No. Description Qty Unit price (RM) Amount (RM)
1 Deep cleaning, 3-room condo (2 cleaners) 1 480.00 480.00
2 Kitchen cabinet and hood degreasing 1 120.00 120.00

Customer provides visitor parking; otherwise parking is charged at cost. Valid 14 days. Payment on completion.

In all 3 examples, every item is on its own line, free items are visible, and the terms answer the question the customer would otherwise ask on WhatsApp.

5 mistakes that cost you money

  1. No valid-until date. Material prices move; your old price should not be forever.
  2. A lump sum only. "Total RM 3,000" invites haggling; itemised lines show where the money goes.
  3. Forgetting the small costs: transport, parking, disposal, wastage, outstation. Either charge them or show them as FOC so the customer knows.
  4. Adding SST without being registered. Only registered businesses may charge it.
  5. No deposit term. Write it down before you buy materials or block a date.

FAQ

Is a quotation legally binding in Malaysia?

A quotation is normally treated as an offer. Once the customer accepts it, for example by signing it or confirming in writing on WhatsApp, it can become a binding agreement under the Contracts Act 1950. A clear scope and validity date protect both sides. This is general information, not legal advice.

How long should a quotation be valid?

7 to 30 days is common. 14 days suits most small jobs; use a shorter period when your material prices change often, and a longer one for events booked months ahead.

Should I add SST to my quotation?

Only if you are registered with JKDM. Charging SST without registration is not allowed; if you are registered, show it as its own line with your SST number.

Do I need an SSM number on my quotation?

Yes, if your business is registered with SSM, whether it is a company (Sdn Bhd), a sole proprietorship or a partnership. For sole proprietors and partnerships, the Registration of Businesses Rules 1957 require the number on every letterhead, invoice, bill or other document that carries the business name. Companies have the same duty under the Companies Act 2016.

Can I send a quotation on WhatsApp?

Yes. Send it as a PDF rather than a typed message or a photo, so the items, totals and terms stay together. Keep the customer's "OK" reply as written acceptance.

Is a quotation the same as an e-Invoice?

No. A quotation comes before any bill and is not submitted to LHDN. e-Invoice rules apply to invoices and related documents, depending on your business; check LHDN's guidance for your situation.
