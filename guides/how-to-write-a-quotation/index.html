How to write a quotation in Malaysia (with examples)

What to put on a quotation in Malaysia: SSM no., SST, validity and payment terms, with an example.

Invois team, EONGERRA TECH

5 min read · Last updated 6 November 2026

In short
  • Put these on it: your business details, the customer, a number and valid-until date, the items, the total, payment terms, what is included, and a way to accept.
  • A validity of 7 to 30 days is common; 14 days suits most small jobs.
  • Add SST only if you are registered with Customs (JKDM).
  • A signature or a WhatsApp "OK" from the customer is your written acceptance, so keep it.

A quotation (in Malay sebut harga, in Chinese 报价单) is a written price offer you give a customer before you start a job. It does not ask for payment: you send an invoice once the customer accepts and the work is due.

A clear quotation wins more jobs than a bare total on WhatsApp and stops arguments later, whether you fix sinks, shoot weddings or give tuition. Not sure which document you need? See quotation vs invoice vs receipt.

What to put on a quotation in Malaysia

  1. Your business details. Name, address, phone and email. If you are registered with SSM, the law requires your registration number on it, such as 202403012345 (do I need to register?).
  2. The customer. Their name and phone, and the address if the work happens there. For a company, add the person to contact ("Attn: Ms Tan, Facilities").
  3. Number and dates. Write QUOTATION at the top and give it a number, such as Q-2611-004; number them in order and never reuse one. Add the date and a valid until date: 14 days suits most small jobs. Without one, a customer can come back months later and ask for last year's price.
  4. The items. One line per item, with the quantity, unit (unit, sqft, hour, package), unit price and amount: a bare total invites haggling. Split labour and materials when the customer is likely to compare, and list small costs such as transport or disposal. Put anything free at RM 0.00 marked FOC, so the customer sees what you included.
  5. Total and SST. Subtotal, discount and total in RM with 2 decimals. If you are registered with Customs (JKDM) for SST, show SST on its own line with its rate and your SST registration number. If not, do not add SST (do I need to charge SST?).
  6. Payment terms. The deposit, when the balance is due, instalments for a long job, and how to pay (bank account or DuitNow QR). For example: "30% deposit to confirm, balance on completion." Agree it before you buy materials or block a date.
  7. What is included. What the price covers and what it does not, the warranty, what happens if the job turns out bigger than expected, and cancellation. Plain sentences work best; there are 20 examples in quotation terms and conditions.
  8. A way to accept. Add a signature line, or write: "To accept, sign and return, or reply 'OK' on WhatsApp." Send it as a PDF and keep the reply: it is your written acceptance.

The lines are quicker to write from a price list you set up once.

Example: a wedding photographer's quotation

Farah Photography, a freelance wedding photographer in Kuala Lumpur registered with SSM, quotes a couple for a pre-wedding shoot in Melaka. Weddings are booked months ahead, so the price stays valid for 30 days.

ToTan Mei Ling, Petaling Jaya, Selangor
Quotation no.Q-2611-004
Date10 November 2026

FAQ

Is a quotation legally binding in Malaysia?

A quotation is normally treated as an offer. Once the customer accepts it, for example by signing it or replying "OK" on WhatsApp, it can become a binding agreement under the Contracts Act 1950. This is general information, not legal advice.

Does a quotation need a signature?

Not normally. Under the Contracts Act 1950, the customer can accept in any usual and reasonable way unless your quotation sets one, so a written "OK" on WhatsApp works too. Keep the signed copy or the reply.

How long should a quotation be valid?

Use a shorter period when your material prices change often, and a longer one for events booked months ahead. Once the date passes, the offer lapses and the customer can no longer accept that price.

Do I need an SSM number on my quotation?

Yes, if your business is registered with SSM, whether it is a sole proprietorship, a partnership or a company (Sdn Bhd). The number goes on every letterhead, invoice, bill or other document that carries the business name.

Is a quotation the same as an e-Invoice?

No. A quotation is not submitted to LHDN: e-Invoice covers invoices, and businesses with annual turnover or revenue under RM 3,000,000 are currently exempt, with a few exceptions.

Sources

  • SSM number on business documents: Registration of Businesses Rules 1957, rule 13; Companies Act 2016.
  • Offer, acceptance and validity: Contracts Act 1950, sections 2, 6 and 7.
  • No SST unless registered: Service Tax Act 2018, section 21.
  • e-Invoice exemption: LHDN, e-Invoice Guideline, Version 4.8 (30 August 2026), section 1.6.1(e).
