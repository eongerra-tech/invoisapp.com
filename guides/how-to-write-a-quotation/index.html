How to write a quotation in Malaysia (with a free template)

Last updated 13 October 2026 · Invois team, Eongerra Tech

A quotation (in Malay sebut harga, in Chinese 报价单) is a written price offer you give a customer before you start a job. It lists the work item by item, the total, how long the price is valid, and how you want to be paid. It is not a bill: the customer owes you nothing until they accept it.

Whether you fix sinks, shoot weddings, clean houses or teach tuition, a clear quotation does three things: it wins more jobs than a bare total on WhatsApp, it stops arguments later about what was included, and it makes your invoice easy to write. This guide covers what must be on it in Malaysia, three worked examples and a template you can copy.

Quotation vs invoice vs receipt

The three documents follow one job from start to finish.

Quotation Invoice Receipt
When Before the work When payment is due (deposit, progress or final) After you are paid
What it says "If you agree, this is the price" "Please pay this amount by this date" "I received this amount, thank you"
Key details Items, total, valid-until date, terms Invoice no., due date, amount owed, bank details Receipt no., amount paid, date, balance (if any)
Usual title QUOTATION INVOICE OFFICIAL RECEIPT

Keep the same customer details and item lines across all three. When the customer accepts a quotation, the invoice should match it unless you both agreed on a change.

What to put on a quotation in Malaysia

1. Your business details

Business name, address, phone and email. If you are registered with SSM, add your registration number (the new format is 12 digits, for example 202401012345). Companies must show their registered name and number on business documents; sole proprietors and partnerships usually include it too, and corporate customers often will not process a quotation without it.

2. Customer and site

Customer name (and company, if they are buying for one), phone, and the site address if the work happens there. For a company, add the person to contact ("Attn: Ms Tan, Facilities").

3. Title, number and dates

The word QUOTATION at the top, a quotation number you can find later (for example Q-2610-001), the date, and a valid until date. Validity of 7 to 30 days is common; 14 days is a sensible default. Without it, a customer can come back months later and ask for last year's price.

4. Itemised lines

One line per item: description, quantity, unit (unit, sqft, hour, package), unit price and amount. Split labour and materials when the customer is likely to compare. If you give something free, list it at RM 0.00 and mark it FOC: the customer sees the favour instead of not knowing it existed.

5. Totals and SST

Subtotal, discount (if any) and total, in RM with two decimals (RM 1,234.50).

Only add SST if you are registered with the Royal Malaysian Customs Department (JKDM) for sales tax or service tax. If you are, show SST as its own line with the rate that applies to your goods or service, and print your SST registration number. If you are not registered, do not add SST at all. Registration is generally required once taxable sales or services pass RM 500,000 a year, and some services have different rules, so check the MySST portal for yours.

6. Payment terms

Deposit (percentage or amount), when the balance is due, instalments if the job is long, and how to pay (bank name, account name and number, or DuitNow QR). For example: "30% deposit to confirm, balance on completion."

7. Scope and terms

What is included, what is not included, warranty, what happens if the job changes once you open the wall or see the site, and cancellation. Short, plain sentences work better than legal-sounding ones.

8. Acceptance

A signature line, or simply: "To accept, sign and return, or reply 'OK' on WhatsApp." Keep that reply: it is your written acceptance.

No. Description Qty Unit price (RM) Amount (RM)

Five mistakes that cost you money

  1. No valid-until date. Material prices move; your old price should not be forever.
  2. A lump sum only. "Total RM 3,000" invites haggling; itemised lines show where the money goes.
  3. Forgetting the small costs: transport, parking, disposal, wastage, outstation. Either charge them or show them as FOC so the customer knows.
  4. Adding SST without being registered. Only registered businesses may charge it.
  5. No deposit term. Write it down before you buy materials or block a date.

FAQ

Is a quotation legally binding in Malaysia?

A quotation is an offer. Once the customer accepts it, for example by signing it or confirming in writing on WhatsApp, it can become a binding agreement under the Contracts Act 1950. A clear scope and validity date protect both sides. This is general information, not legal advice.

How long should a quotation be valid?

Seven to 30 days is common. Fourteen days suits most small jobs; use a shorter period when your material prices change often, and a longer one for events booked months ahead.

Should I add SST to my quotation?

Only if you are registered with JKDM for sales tax or service tax. Then show SST as a separate line with your SST registration number. If you are not registered, you must not charge SST.

Do I need an SSM number on my quotation?

Companies must show their registered name and number on business documents. If you are a registered sole proprietor or partnership, adding your SSM number is good practice and many corporate customers expect it.

Can I send a quotation on WhatsApp?

Yes. Send it as a PDF rather than a typed message or a photo, so the items, totals and terms stay together. Keep the customer's "OK" reply as written acceptance.

Is a quotation the same as an e-Invoice?

No. A quotation is an offer and is not submitted to LHDN. e-Invoice rules apply to invoices and related documents, depending on your business; check LHDN's guidance for your situation. Invois does not submit e-Invoices.
