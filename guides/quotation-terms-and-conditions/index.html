Quotation terms and conditions: 20 examples (deposit, warranty, cancellation)

20 quotation terms you can copy for deposits, payment, scope, cancellation and warranty, with notes on when to use them and what the law says about deposits.

Invois team, EONGERRA TECH

8 min read · Last updated 23 October 2026 · General information, not legal advice

In short
  • Pick 5 to 8 terms; a long list usually goes unread.
  • Say how you get paid, how long the price holds and what it covers; add cancellation and warranty terms where they fit.
  • Keep a non-refundable deposit close to your real costs; a court can reduce one that is far higher.
  • If your customer is a consumer, your terms cannot take away their rights under the Consumer Protection Act 1999.

Terms are the few lines at the bottom of a quotation: how you get paid, what the price covers, and what happens if something changes.

Once the customer accepts the quotation, these lines normally become part of what you both agreed. Short, clear terms help avoid arguments later.

Below are 20 terms ready to copy: 16 by topic and 4 for particular kinds of work. Everything else a quotation must show is in How to write a quotation.

How to use these terms

  • Pick 5 to 8. A long list of terms usually goes unread.
  • Check the numbers: the deposit you actually take, and a warranty you can keep.
  • Use the same terms on the invoice, so nothing changes between the quotation and the invoice.

Deposit and payment

  1. "A deposit of 50% is payable to confirm the booking. Work starts once the deposit is received."When to use: jobs where you block a date or buy materials before you start.
  2. "The balance is due on the day the work is completed."When to use: short jobs finished in 1 visit or 1 day.
  3. "For jobs above RM 5,000.00, payment is in 3 parts: 30% deposit, 40% at the halfway point and 30% on completion."When to use: longer jobs, so you are not paying for the whole job out of your own pocket.
    For a RM 6,000.00 job: RM 1,800.00 + RM 2,400.00 + RM 1,800.00 = RM 6,000.00.
  4. "Invoices are due within 14 days of the invoice date. The next stage starts after the previous stage is paid."When to use: staged work, or business customers who pay against an invoice.

Validity and price

  1. "This quotation is valid for 14 days from the date above."When to use: always. After that date, you can send a new quotation if your costs have changed.
  2. "Prices are based on material prices on the date of this quotation. If the job starts after the validity date, we will send a revised quotation for your approval before work starts."When to use: jobs that depend on materials whose prices move often.
  3. "Outstation jobs more than 30 km from Kuala Lumpur carry a transport charge of RM 50.00."When to use: jobs at a customer's location far from your base. Put in your own town and distance.

Scope and extra work

  1. "The price covers only the items listed above. Anything not listed is quoted separately."When to use: always. It avoids the "I thought that was included" argument.
  2. "Any change or extra work requested after acceptance will be quoted in writing and agreed before it starts."When to use: jobs where customers often add things while the work is going on.
  3. "If damage that could not be seen when we quoted is found once work starts, we will stop, show you, and quote before continuing."When to use: repairs and renovation, where the real condition only shows once work starts.
  4. "The price includes 2 rounds of changes. Further changes are charged at RM 80.00 per hour."When to use: design, photography, video and writing, where the customer reviews the work and asks for changes.

Cancellation and date changes

  1. "If you cancel more than 14 days before the agreed date, the deposit is refunded less any costs already paid for this job."
  2. "If you cancel within 14 days of the agreed date, the deposit covers the date we reserved and the materials already bought, and is not refunded."
  3. "The date can be changed once at no charge if the change is made at least 7 days before the agreed date. Later changes depend on availability."When to use: bookings for a fixed date, such as events, makeup, photography or a move-out clean.

What the law says about keeping a deposit

If the customer cancels without a valid reason:

  • You can normally keep the deposit, up to the amount written, if your quotation says clearly that it is not refundable. Under the Contracts Act, as the Federal Court reads it, it is then up to the customer to show that the amount is unreasonable.
  • The amount must still be reasonable. A court can reduce a deposit that is far larger than the loss you were likely to suffer.
  • If your customer is a consumer (see Warranty below) and the claim is RM 50,000 or less, the Tribunal for Consumer Claims can also declare an unfair penalty term unenforceable.
  • An advance payment that is only part of the price is not a deposit, even if you call it a "deposit". The customer can then usually claim it back.

So tie the deposit to real costs, as term 13 does: the date you reserved and the materials you have already bought. Do not set it far above those costs.

References: Contracts Act 1950, section 75; Federal Court, Cubic Electronics v Mars Telecommunications, 2018; Consumer Protection Act 1999, sections 24D, 24G and 98.

Warranty

  1. "Our workmanship is guaranteed for 12 months from completion. We will fix any defect in our work at no charge during this period. This is in addition to your rights under the Consumer Protection Act 1999."
  2. "The warranty does not cover damage from misuse, accidents, or work done by others after we finish."When to use: repairs, installation and renovation. Offer only a period you can honour.

When your customer is a consumer

The Consumer Protection Act 1999 protects consumers. Your customer is usually one if they are buying a kind of service normally bought for personal or household use. The Act does not cover professionals regulated by their own law, such as lawyers and architects.

A consumer already has these rights under the Act:

  • The work is done with reasonable care and skill.
  • Materials you supply are fit for their purpose.
  • The work is finished within a reasonable time, if no date was agreed.

What this means for your terms:

  • Your terms cannot remove or reduce these rights, and trying to do so is also an offence.
  • Your warranty comes on top of these rights. That is why term 15 mentions them.
  • A court or the Tribunal can declare a one-sided term unenforceable. One example is a clause saying you are not responsible for your own negligence.

References: Consumer Protection Act 1999, sections 2, 3, 6, 24D, 24G, 53 and 55.

4 terms for particular kinds of work

  1. Cleaning "Cleaning supplies are included. Mould or hard stains are quoted separately after inspection."
  2. Makeup "A trial session is charged separately and deducted from the package if you book within 30 days of the trial."
  3. Roofing "Days lost to rain are added to the completion date."
  4. Tuition "Fees are paid at the start of each month. A missed class is replaced in the same month if you tell us at least 24 hours before the class."

FAQ

Can I write my terms in Bahasa Melayu or Chinese?

Yes, as long as the meaning is clear. Use the language your customer reads best.

Can I keep the whole deposit if the customer cancels?

Usually, but only up to an amount that is reasonable against your loss (see Cancellation above). Keep proof of your costs: material receipts, and messages from other bookings you turned down for that date. This helps if the customer disputes the deposit.

How much deposit should I ask for?

There is no set percentage. This guide uses 50% (term 1) and 30% (term 3) as examples. Take enough to cover the date you block and the materials you buy before the job starts.

Can I charge interest on late payment?

No general law sets a late-payment interest rate for ordinary invoices. If you want to charge interest, put the rate in the quotation so the customer agrees to it first. A rate that is excessive can be treated as a penalty and reduced (Contracts Act 1950, section 75). If a customer does not pay at all, see Customer not paying.

Where do the terms go on a quotation?

Under the total, so the customer sees them before agreeing to the price. If the customer agrees on WhatsApp, send the quotation as a PDF so the terms arrive with the price.

This is general information, not legal advice. For large or unusual jobs, ask a lawyer to check your terms.
