Quotation terms and conditions: 20 examples (deposit, warranty, cancellation)

20 quotation terms you can copy for deposits, payment, scope, cancellation and warranty, with notes on when to use them and what the law says about deposits.

Invois team, EONGERRA TECH

6 min read · Last updated 6 November 2026

In short
  • Most quotations need: a deposit, when the balance is due, how long the price holds, what is not included, and a cancellation rule.
  • Pick 5 to 8 terms; a long list usually goes unread.
  • Keep a non-refundable deposit close to your real costs; a court can reduce one that is far higher.
  • Home customers are usually protected by the Consumer Protection Act: the work must be done properly and materials must be fit for use. Your terms cannot take that away.

Terms are the few lines at the bottom of a quotation: how you get paid, what the price covers, and what happens if something changes. Once the customer accepts the quotation, they normally become part of what you both agreed.

Below are 20 terms to copy. Everything else a quotation needs is in how to write a quotation.

How to use these terms

  • A starter set for most jobs: terms 1, 2, 5, 8, 9 and 13. Add 15 for repairs and installation.
  • Change the numbers in each term to your own: the deposit, the days and any RM amounts.
  • Use the same terms on the invoice, so nothing changes between the quotation and the invoice.

Deposit and payment

  1. "A deposit of 50% is payable to confirm the booking. Work starts once the deposit is received."When to use: jobs where you block a date or buy materials before you start.
  2. "The balance is due on the day the work is completed."When to use: short jobs finished in 1 visit or 1 day.
  3. "For jobs above RM 5,000.00, payment is in 3 parts: 30% deposit, 40% at the halfway point and 30% on completion."When to use: longer jobs, so you are not paying for the whole job out of your own pocket.
  4. "Invoices are due within 14 days of the invoice date. The next stage starts after the previous stage is paid."When to use: staged work, or business customers who pay against an invoice.

Validity and price

  1. "This quotation is valid for 14 days from the date above."When to use: always.
  2. "Prices are based on material prices on the date of this quotation. If the job starts after the validity date, we will send a revised quotation for your approval before work starts."
  3. "Outstation jobs more than 30 km from Kuala Lumpur carry a transport charge of RM 50.00."When to use: jobs at a customer's location far from your base. Put in your own town and distance.

Scope and extra work

  1. "The price covers only the items listed above. Anything not listed is quoted separately."When to use: always. It avoids the "I thought that was included" argument.
  2. "Any change or extra work requested after acceptance will be quoted in writing and agreed before it starts."
  3. "If damage that could not be seen when we quoted is found once work starts, we will stop, show you, and quote before continuing."When to use: repairs and renovation.
  4. "The price includes 2 rounds of changes. Further changes are charged at RM 80.00 per hour."When to use: design, photography, video and writing.

Cancellation and date changes

  1. "If you cancel more than 14 days before the agreed date, the deposit is refunded less any costs already paid for this job."
  2. "If you cancel within 14 days of the agreed date, the deposit covers the date we reserved and the materials already bought, and is not refunded."
  3. "The date can be changed once at no charge if the change is made at least 7 days before the agreed date. Later changes depend on availability."When to use: bookings for a fixed date, such as events, makeup, photography or a move-out clean.

Warranty

  1. "Our workmanship is guaranteed for 12 months from completion. We will fix any defect in our work at no charge during this period. This is in addition to your rights under the Consumer Protection Act 1999."
  2. "The warranty does not cover damage from misuse, accidents, or work done by others after we finish."When to use: repairs, installation and renovation. Offer only a period you can honour.

4 terms for particular kinds of work

  1. Cleaning "Cleaning supplies are included. Mould or hard stains are quoted separately after inspection."
  2. Makeup "A trial session is charged separately and deducted from the package if you book within 30 days of the trial."
  3. Roofing "Days lost to rain are added to the completion date."
  4. Tuition "Fees are paid at the start of each month. A missed class is replaced in the same month if you tell us at least 24 hours before the class."

What the law says

About keeping a deposit when the customer cancels without a valid reason:

  • You can normally keep it, up to the amount written, if your quotation says clearly that it is not refundable.
  • The amount must be reasonable: a court can reduce a deposit far larger than your likely loss. If the customer disputes it, they have to show it is unreasonable.
  • Money paid only as part of the price can usually be claimed back, even if you call it a deposit; a deposit paid to secure the booking usually cannot. So say what the deposit is for, as term 1 does, and tie it to real costs, as term 13 does.

When your customer is a consumer, usually someone buying for personal or household use:

  • The work must be done properly, materials you supply must be fit for use, and the job finished within a reasonable time if no date was agreed.
  • Your terms cannot remove these rights, and trying to is an offence.
  • A court, or for claims of RM 50,000 or less the Tribunal for Consumer Claims, can rule that an unfair term does not bind the customer, such as a clause saying you are not responsible for your own negligence.

This is general information, not legal advice. For large or unusual jobs, ask a lawyer to check your terms.

FAQ

Can I write my terms in Bahasa Melayu or Chinese?

Yes, as long as the meaning is clear. Use the language your customer reads best.

Can I keep the whole deposit if the customer cancels?

Usually, up to a reasonable amount. Keep proof of your costs, such as material receipts and messages from bookings you turned down for that date, in case the customer disputes it.

How much deposit should I ask for?

No law sets a percentage. Take enough to cover the date you block and the materials you buy before the job starts.

Can I charge interest on late payment?

To charge it yourself, put the rate in the quotation so the customer agrees to it first; no general law sets one for ordinary invoices. A court can reduce an excessive rate as a penalty. If a customer does not pay at all, see customer not paying.

Where do the terms go on a quotation?

Under the total, so the customer sees them before agreeing to the price. If the customer agrees on WhatsApp, send the quotation as a PDF so the terms arrive with the price.

Sources

  • Deposits and penalties: Contracts Act 1950, section 75; Federal Court, Cubic Electronics Sdn Bhd v Mars Telecommunications Sdn Bhd, 21 November 2018, paragraphs 70 and 74.
  • Consumers: Consumer Protection Act 1999, sections 2, 3, 6, 24D, 24G, 53 to 55 and 98.
