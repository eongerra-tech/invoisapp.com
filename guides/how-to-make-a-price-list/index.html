How to make a price list for your services (with an example)

What to put on each line of a price list, how to choose the unit, and an example list.

Invois team, EONGERRA TECH

5 min read · Last updated 6 November 2026

In short
  • Each line needs a name, a unit, a price in RM and a note on what is included.
  • Use 1 unit per line, such as per hour, per sq ft, per package or per month.
  • When the price varies, give each version its own line, such as a half-day and a full-day photo shoot. Use a "from" price only when you can't tell until you see the job, like an aircond gas top-up.
  • Give small costs their own lines: transport, disposal, materials, rush jobs and a minimum charge.

A price list is your own list of what you charge: each service, its unit and its price. You set it up once and price every quotation from it, so the same job gets the same price every time. To turn it into a quotation, see how to write a quotation.

How to make your price list

  1. Write each line in 4 parts. Paper, your phone's notes or a spreadsheet are fine to start.
    • Name: the service, plus anything that changes the price, such as "Website, up to 5 pages".
    • Unit: what the price is for, such as 1 hour or 1 package.
    • Price: the amount in RM, such as RM 150.00.
    • Note: what is included and what is not, such as "Includes 2 rounds of changes. Not included: writing the text and taking the photos."
  2. Pick 1 unit per line. If you sell the same work by the hour and as a package, make them 2 lines.
    • Per unit or per person for things you can count: aircond units, catering per pax.
    • Per sq ft or per metre for work that grows with size: tiling, banner printing.
    • Per hour when you can't tell how long it takes: repairs, video editing.
    • Per package for a fixed bundle: a half-day photo shoot, a 5-page website.
    • Per month for ongoing work: tuition, website hosting.
  3. Split a price that varies. Give each version its own line, such as a half-day and a full-day shoot.
    • Use a "from" price only when you can't tell until you see the job, and say in the note what moves it.
    • A customer who sees "from" tends to expect the lowest number, so give the real price in the quotation before you start.
  4. Give small costs their own lines. If you waive one for a customer, keep its line on the quotation at RM 0.00, so the customer sees what you included at no charge.
    • Transport and outstation trips
    • Disposal of old parts or rubbish
    • Materials you supply
    • Rush jobs and public holidays
    • A minimum charge for very short jobs
  5. Group the lines under a few headings, such as Packages and Add-ons, so you find an item quickly.

The same layout works for any work:

  • A web designer lists "Website, up to 5 pages" per package and "Hosting and maintenance" per month.
  • An aircond technician lists "Chemical wash" per unit and "Gas top-up (R32)" from RM 100.00, depending on how much gas is needed.

FAQ

Is a price list the same as a quotation?

No. A price list holds your usual rates for every customer; a quotation prices one job for one customer, with quantities, a total and a valid-until date.

Should I show my prices to customers?

If most of your jobs are standard, it saves time: customers see the price before they ask. If prices depend on the job, show the "from" prices and what moves them, then give the exact price in a quotation.

Per hour or per job: which is better?

Often both: a package price for the agreed work, so the customer knows the total, plus an hourly rate for anything beyond it.

Should my prices include SST?

If you are SST-registered, keep the list prices before SST, add SST as its own line on the quotation, and mark a shared list "Prices exclude SST". If you are not registered, do not add SST (do I need to charge SST?).

How often should I update my price list?

Update it whenever one of your costs changes, such as a supplier's price or your transport costs. A quotation you have already sent normally keeps its price until its valid-until date, so new prices apply to new quotations.

Sources

  • No SST unless registered: Service Tax Act 2018, section 21.
