What is a purchase order? What to do when a customer sends a PO

A PO is the customer's written order. Here is how it differs from your quotation, what to check before you start, and where its number goes on your invoice.

Invois team, EONGERRA TECH

5 min read · Last updated 11 November 2026

In short
  • A purchase order (PO) is the customer's written order for your goods or services. It usually comes after your quotation and before your invoice.
  • Check it against your quotation before you start: the items, quantities, prices, total, dates and payment terms.
  • If something differs, settle it in writing first. Starting the work can count as agreeing to the PO's version.
  • Put the PO number on your invoice. Many accounts teams need it to match your invoice and pay you.

What is a purchase order?

A purchase order (in Malay pesanan belian, in Chinese 采购单) is how a company, school or government office orders goods or services from you. Someone there usually approves the spending before the PO is sent, so it is their go-ahead.

A PO usually shows:

  • the PO number and date;
  • the customer's company name and address, and where to deliver;
  • the items, quantities, unit prices and total;
  • the date they need it by, and the payment terms;
  • the signature of the person who approved it;
  • sometimes the customer's own terms and conditions.

Quotation, PO, then invoice

Aina Studio, a freelance graphic designer in Puchong, quotes a café company for a logo job. The same job is the example in our invoice guide.

  1. 20 OctoberQuotation Q-2610-018: RM 1,990.00, valid until 3 November
  2. 23 OctoberThe café company sends PO 4471
  3. 7 NovemberAina Studio delivers the files
  4. 10 NovemberInvoice INV-2611-003, with the PO number on it
  5. 10 DecemberPayment due (Net 30), then a receipt

Your quotation is normally treated as an offer. A PO that orders exactly what you quoted is the customer's acceptance in writing, so from then on the deal normally binds you both.

Before you start, check it against your quotation:

  1. Items and quantities: the same as you quoted.
  2. Prices and total: the same unit prices and the same total.
  3. Dates: the date they need it by is one you can meet.
  4. Payment terms: the deposit and the days you quoted.
  5. Their terms: read any printed on the PO, such as a charge for late delivery.
  6. The company name: bill the company named on the PO, not the person who sent it.

PO 4471 matches the quotation line by line, so Aina Studio replies before starting:

Reply: "Thank you, PO 4471 received. The logo, business cards and templates will be ready by 7 November."

If the PO differs from your quotation

Suppose the PO had said payment 60 days from the invoice date, while Aina Studio quoted 30.

A PO that changes your quotation is normally not an acceptance of it: under the Contracts Act 1950, an acceptance must take the offer as it stands. It normally counts as the customer's new offer instead.

If you start the work without a word, you can be taken to have accepted the PO as written, 60 days included. So reply in writing before you start: ask for a revised PO, or agree to the change if it works for you.

Reply: "Thank you for PO 4471. Our quotation Q-2610-018 is for payment 30 days from the invoice date. Could you send a revised PO with 30 days?"

The same goes for a different price, a missing item or an earlier date. A PO that arrives after your quotation's valid-until date is a new offer too: you can accept it or quote again.

Put the PO number on your invoice

When the work is done, write the PO number on your invoice, usually as "Your Ref." or "PO No.". Many accounts teams match each invoice to its PO before they pay, and may hold an invoice without the number.

Aina Studio's invoice INV-2611-003 shows "Your Ref. PO 4471", the same 3 items and RM 1,990.00, due on 10 December.

FAQ

What if a customer sends a PO without a quotation?

Then the PO is normally their offer, for example to buy items from your price list. Once you accept it, by confirming or simply by doing the work, it can bind you both (Contracts Act 1950, sections 2 and 8).

Can I start work without a PO?

Yes: a written OK on your quotation, even on WhatsApp, is normally enough to agree the job. But if the company pays only against a PO, ask for one first, or your invoice may be held.

The customer changed the order after the PO. What now?

Ask for a revised PO, or a written change that quotes the PO number, before you do the extra work. On the invoice, put the change on its own line.

Can the customer cancel a PO?

Not on their own once a PO that matches your quotation reaches you: cancelling then normally needs your agreement, unless the agreed terms allow it (Contracts Act 1950, sections 4 and 5). A cancellation term in your quotation settles what happens.

What is a government LO or LPO?

An LO or LPO (Pesanan Kerajaan) is a government office's PO. Return a paper one's original with your invoice: the department cannot pay without it, and must pay within 14 days of receiving a complete, correct bill (AP 169D(d), 103(a)).

Sources

  • Offer, acceptance and cancelling: Contracts Act 1950 (reprint to 1 January 2006), sections 2 and 4 to 8.
  • Government orders: Ministry of Finance, Arahan Perbendaharaan (Pindaan 2023), AP 103(a) and 169D(d).
