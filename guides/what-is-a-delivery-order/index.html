What is a delivery order? What goes on a DO, and how it links to your invoice

A DO goes with your goods; the invoice asks for payment. Here is what to put on a DO, why to keep the signed copy, and where its number goes on your invoice.

Invois team, EONGERRA TECH

5 min read · Last updated 18 November 2026

In short
  • A delivery order (DO) is the note that goes with your goods. It shows what you delivered, to whom and when. It usually has no prices: your invoice is what asks for payment.
  • A DO usually shows: your business name, a DO number and date, the customer's name and delivery address, their PO number if they sent one, the items and quantities, and the receiver's name, signature and date.
  • Keep the signed copy. It is your proof that the goods arrived if the customer later denies it or does not pay.
  • Put the DO number on your invoice, next to the PO number. It helps the customer's accounts team match your invoice to the order and the delivery before they pay.

What is a delivery order?

In Malaysia, a delivery order (DO) usually means the note that goes with goods you deliver, such as printed banners, a catering order, furniture or spare parts. Someone at the customer's place signs it to say the goods arrived.

The DO usually comes after the customer's purchase order (PO) and before or with your invoice. Siti Prints, a small print studio in Bangi, prints banners and flyers for Minda Ceria Learning Sdn Bhd, a tuition centre in Kajang:

  1. 30 OctoberQuotation Q-2610-024: 2 banners and 2,000 flyers, RM 540.00
  2. 3 NovemberMinda Ceria sends PO 2210
  3. 6 NovemberDO-0151 with the 2 banners, signed
  4. 10 NovemberDO-0152 with the 2,000 flyers, signed
  5. 11 NovemberInvoice INV-2611-012, with the PO and both DO numbers

What goes on a DO

This is the second DO Siti Prints sent:

DO no.DO-0152
Date10 November 2026
FromSiti Prints, Bangi, Selangor
Deliver toMinda Ceria Learning Sdn Bhd, Kajang, Selangor
Your PO no.2210
ItemA5 flyers, full colour, both sides
Quantity2,000
Received byNurul (office admin), signed and stamped, 10 November 2026

There are no prices on it. The person who receives the goods only needs to check what arrived; the prices go on your invoice.

Why keep the signed copy

A signed DO shows that the goods reached the customer, when, and who took them. Keep it with the invoice: it is your proof if the customer later says something never arrived, or does not pay.

A signature at the door is not always final. Under the Sale of Goods Act 1957, a customer is not treated as having accepted goods until they have had a reasonable chance to check them.

If they keep the goods beyond a reasonable time without telling you they reject them, they are treated as having accepted them.

So when you can, count and check the goods with the person receiving them, and write any shortage or damage on the DO before you both sign.

Part deliveries

The banners were ready first, and Minda Ceria agreed on WhatsApp to take them on 6 November. A customer does not have to take an order in parts unless they agreed to it, so keep that message.

Each delivery gets its own DO and number. Suppose only 1,500 flyers had been ready on 10 November. Write 1,500 on the DO, not 2,000, and tell the customer when the other 500 will come.

Unless you agreed otherwise, they may refuse a short delivery. If they accept it, they pay for those 1,500 flyers at the quoted price per flyer.

Put the DO number on your invoice

When everything is delivered, write the DO numbers on your invoice, next to the PO number. Many accounts teams match each invoice to the order and the delivery before they pay, and the 2 numbers help them find both.

One invoice can cover several DOs. Siti Prints' invoice INV-2611-012 lists the banners and the flyers, RM 540.00, with PO 2210 under Your Ref. and both DO numbers under Our D/O No.

FAQ

Do I need a DO for a service?

A DO is for goods you hand over. For a service such as a repair or a design job, the invoice describes the work; keep the customer's written OK on the finished job as your proof.

Can I ask for payment when I deliver?

Yes: unless you agreed otherwise, such as credit terms, the goods and the payment are meant to change hands together (Sale of Goods Act 1957, section 32). Bring the invoice with the DO, or a cash bill if they pay on the spot.

What if the customer is not there to sign?

Delivery counts once the goods are in the hands of the customer or someone they authorised, or done the way you both agreed (Sale of Goods Act 1957, section 33). Agree beforehand who may sign, such as the reception, and get their name on the DO.

What is a GRN?

A goods received note (GRN) is the customer's own record of what arrived, made after they check your delivery. Larger customers often match it with their PO and your invoice before they pay.

What is a DO from a shipping line?

That is a different paper. When goods come in by sea, the shipping line or its agent issues a DO that lets the importer collect the cargo at the port.

Sources
