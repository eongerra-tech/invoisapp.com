Quotation vs invoice vs receipt: what's the difference in Malaysia?

Last updated 13 October 2026 · Invois team, Eongerra Tech

A quotation says what a job will cost if the customer agrees. An invoice asks the customer to pay. A receipt confirms you were paid. Mix them up and deposits go missing, or customers pay twice.

This guide puts the three side by side, then follows one job from quote to the last ringgit, deposit and balance included, with the maths shown. The examples are an event caterer, an electrician and a tutor; the flow is the same whether you cut hair, fix pipes or shoot weddings.

The difference in one table

Quotation Invoice Receipt
When Before the work When a payment is due After money comes in
What it says "If you agree, this is the price" "Please pay this amount by this date" "I received this amount"
Does the customer owe you? Not yet Yes Less, or nothing
Key details Items, total, valid-until date, terms Invoice no., due date, amount, bank details Receipt no., amount paid, date, balance left
How many per job One (or a revised one) Usually one; sometimes one per stage One per payment
Usual title QUOTATION INVOICE (TAX INVOICE if SST-registered) OFFICIAL RECEIPT

Quick test: if you have not asked for money yet, it is a quotation; if you are asking for money, it is an invoice; if the money has arrived, it is a receipt. For the first one, see How to write a quotation in Malaysia.

One job from quotation to receipt

An event caterer is asked to do a family-day buffet. Prices are made up to show the format, not a price guide.

Step 1: the quotation (3 Nov)

No. Description Qty Unit price (RM) Amount (RM)
1 Buffet menu B 100 pax 22.00 2,200.00
2 Canopy with tables and chairs 2 sets 180.00 360.00
3 Servers (3 staff, 5 hours) 1 300.00 300.00
4 Transport and setup 1 120.00 120.00
5 Mineral water 100 bottles FOC 0.00
Total RM 2,980.00

Quotation Q-2611-007, valid until 17 Nov. Terms: 30% deposit to confirm the date, balance on the event day. The caterer is not SST-registered, so there is no SST line.

Step 2: the invoice (5 Nov)

The customer replies "OK, confirm" on WhatsApp. Now the caterer issues invoice INV-2611-012 for the same items and the same RM 2,980.00, with the payment terms spelled out:

  • Deposit 30%: RM 894.00, due 12 Nov (2,980.00 x 30%)
  • Balance: RM 2,086.00, due on 29 Nov, the event day (2,980.00 - 894.00)

One invoice with the deposit and balance on it keeps the numbers in one place. A separate deposit invoice plus a final invoice also works, as long as the final one deducts the deposit.

Step 3: the deposit and the first receipt (10 Nov)

RM 894.00 arrives by bank transfer. The caterer issues receipt OR-0031:

Invoice no. Invoice total Paid now Outstanding
INV-2611-012 RM 2,980.00 RM 894.00 RM 2,086.00

The customer keeps this as proof the date is booked. The invoice does not change; it now has RM 894.00 paid against it.

Step 4: the balance and the final receipt (29 Nov)

After the event, the customer pays the balance by DuitNow. Receipt OR-0035:

Invoice no. Invoice total Paid now Outstanding
INV-2611-012 RM 2,980.00 RM 2,086.00 RM 0.00

RM 894.00 + RM 2,086.00 = RM 2,980.00, exactly the invoice total. The receipt numbers skip because other jobs were paid in between; what matters is that each number is used once.

If the job changes

Suppose that, a week before the event, the headcount had gone up to 120 pax. Send a revised quotation or update the invoice first, so the paperwork matches what was agreed. Buffet: 120 x RM 22.00 = RM 2,640.00, new total RM 3,420.00. The deposit already paid stays RM 894.00, so the balance becomes RM 3,420.00 - RM 894.00 = RM 2,526.00, and the final receipt would show RM 2,526.00 paid.

2 more ways it plays out

Electrician: paid on the spot, or within 30 days

A homeowner pays the electrician RM 380.00 by DuitNow for a new RCCB and 2 plug points before the van leaves. The invoice and receipt go out together, or one invoice marked PAID. The customer still gets a numbered document, which matters the day a warranty question comes up.

For a company customer, the accounts department wants an invoice with your SSM number first and pays within, say, 30 days ("Net 30"). The receipt follows when the money is in.

Tutor: no quotation, an invoice every month

A tutor charges RM 320.00 a month; the fee was agreed once, so no monthly quotation. On the 1st of each month the tutor sends an invoice due on the 7th, and issues a receipt when the parent pays. If the parent pays RM 160.00 now and RM 160.00 next week, that is 2 receipts against one invoice, the first showing RM 160.00 outstanding.

What must be on an invoice and a receipt

Invoice checklist

  • The word INVOICE and an invoice number in one running sequence (INV-2611-012)
  • Invoice date and due date
  • Your business name, address and phone; SSM registration number if you are registered
  • Customer name (and company, if a company pays) and the quotation or PO number it relates to
  • Item lines matching the accepted quotation, subtotal, discount, total in RM
  • SST as its own line, with your SST number, only if you are registered with JKDM. Not registered: no SST at all (rules on the MySST portal)
  • Deposit and balance amounts, or the instalment dates
  • Bank details: bank, account name, account number, or a DuitNow QR

Receipt checklist

  • The word RECEIPT (often OFFICIAL RECEIPT) and a receipt number in its own running sequence
  • Date the money was received
  • Your business name and SSM number (if registered)
  • Who paid ("Received from")
  • The invoice number it pays, and the amount received, in figures and ideally in words
  • How it was paid, with the transfer reference or cheque number
  • Balance still outstanding after this payment (RM 0.00 when fully paid)

What "official receipt" means

Many Malaysian businesses title their receipts OFFICIAL RECEIPT: a numbered, formal acknowledgement rather than a handwritten note. What makes it useful is the checklist above and the numbering: never reuse a number; if you make a mistake, mark that receipt CANCELLED and keep the copy.

Keep your invoices and receipts for at least 7 years. Section 82 of the Income Tax Act 1967 requires every business, from sole proprietors and partnerships to companies (Sdn Bhd), to keep its records for 7 years from the end of the year the income relates to (LHDN Public Rulings 4/2000 (Revised) for companies and 5/2000 (Revised) for individuals and partnerships). Whether e-Invoice rules also apply to you depends on your business; check LHDN's guidance.

FAQ

Can a receipt replace an invoice?

For a small job paid on the spot, many businesses issue only a receipt, or an invoice marked PAID. When the customer pays later, or a company is paying, send an invoice first: it is what their accounts department pays against.

Should I give a receipt for a deposit?

Yes. Issue a receipt for every payment, deposit included, showing the balance still outstanding. It is the customer's proof that the date or order is confirmed.

Can I issue one invoice for the deposit and the balance?

Yes. List the deposit and the balance, each with its due date, on one invoice, then issue a receipt for each payment as it comes in. If the customer's accounts department wants a separate invoice for each payment, issue a deposit invoice and a final invoice that deducts the deposit.

Can I add SST to my invoice if I am not registered?

No. Only businesses registered with JKDM for sales tax or service tax may charge SST. If you are registered, show SST as a separate line with your SST registration number.

How long should I keep invoices and receipts?

At least 7 years. Section 82 of the Income Tax Act 1967 requires sole proprietors, partnerships and companies alike to keep their business records, invoices and receipts included, for 7 years. If a tax return is filed late, the 7 years run from the end of the year it is filed.

Is an invoice the same as an e-Invoice?

No. An invoice is the bill you send your customer. e-Invoice is LHDN's system for validating certain invoices, and whether it applies depends on your business; check LHDN's guidance. Invois does not submit e-Invoices.
