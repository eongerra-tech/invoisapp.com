What is a proforma invoice? What to send when a customer asks for one

A proforma invoice shows what your invoice will say, often so a company can pay a deposit first. Here is how it differs from a quotation and an invoice, and what comes next.

Invois team, EONGERRA TECH

5 min read · Last updated 18 November 2026

In short
  • A proforma invoice shows what your invoice will say: the items, prices and payment terms of a sale that has not happened yet. It is not your invoice; the real one comes later, with its own number.
  • Customers usually ask for one to get the spending approved, or to pay a deposit before the work or delivery.
  • A quotation is your offer, made before the customer agrees. A proforma usually comes after they agree, laid out like the invoice to come.
  • If they pay against it, give a receipt for that payment. Your invoice then shows the full total, the amount already paid and the balance.

What is a proforma invoice?

A proforma invoice is a draft of the invoice you will send, made before the sale goes ahead. It lists the same items, prices and payment terms, under the title PROFORMA INVOICE.

It is not your invoice. When the sale goes ahead, you still send the real invoice, with a number from your usual invoice series.

Why customers ask for one

There are usually 2 reasons:

  • their finance or purchasing team needs a document laid out like an invoice before it approves the spending;
  • they will pay a deposit, or the whole amount, before the work starts or the goods arrive.

Haziq Films, a freelance videographer in Shah Alam, quotes a property company for a video of its annual dinner. The company agrees, and its finance team asks for a proforma invoice so it can pay a 50% deposit first:

  1. 13 OctoberQuotation Q-2610-009: RM 3,000.00, 50% deposit
  2. 16 OctoberThe company agrees by email
  3. 20 OctoberProforma invoice PF-2610-001 for the deposit
  4. 23 OctoberDeposit of RM 1,500.00 paid, receipt sent
  5. 31 OctoberAnnual dinner filmed
  6. 3 NovemberInvoice INV-2611-001: RM 3,000.00, RM 1,500.00 still due

Proforma invoice or quotation?

Your quotation is your offer: the customer may still ask for changes, and it holds until its valid-until date. A proforma usually comes once they have agreed, and is laid out like the invoice that will follow.

If the customer only needs a price to approve, ask whether your quotation will do. Some finance teams accept a quotation that shows the deposit; others want a document titled proforma invoice.

It carries the same details as your invoice, including how to pay you, with 3 differences: the title says PROFORMA INVOICE, its number comes from a separate series, and a note says it is not an invoice.

After the customer pays

When the deposit arrives, give a receipt for it that names the proforma number. After the job, send the invoice for the full amount, showing the deposit already paid and the balance:

FAQ

Can I send an invoice for the deposit instead?

Yes, if their finance team accepts it: send the invoice for the full amount before the job, with the deposit due first. Once the deposit is paid, the same invoice shows it as paid and the balance still due.

Is a proforma invoice legally binding?

If the customer accepted your quotation, that deal can bind you both; the proforma just asks for payment. Sent on its own, a proforma is normally an offer, and paying its deposit can count as accepting it (Contracts Act 1950, sections 2 and 8).

What if the price changes after the proforma?

Send a revised proforma with the new figures and get the customer's OK on it. Your invoice follows what was finally agreed.

Is a proforma invoice an e-Invoice?

No. LHDN accepts an e-Invoice only once it is validated, and a proforma comes before that. If your business must issue e-Invoices, LHDN wants one for a non-refundable deposit too.

Can a proforma invoice use my invoice numbers?

Better not. Give proformas their own series, such as PF-2610-001, so your invoice numbers run in order with no gaps for sales that never happened.

Sources

  • Offer and acceptance: Contracts Act 1950 (reprint to 1 January 2006), sections 2 and 8.
  • e-Invoice: LHDN, e-Invoice General FAQs (updated 4 September 2026), questions 40 and 57.
