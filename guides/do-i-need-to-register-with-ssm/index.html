Do I need to register with SSM?

If you work for yourself in Peninsular Malaysia or Labuan, you normally must register within 30 days, from RM 30 a year. Here is who is exempt and how to apply.

Invois team, EONGERRA TECH

7 min read · Last updated 31 October 2026

In short
  • Who: in Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan, if you work for yourself to earn money, you normally have to register, even for a side job. There is no minimum income. Sabah and Sarawak use their own laws.
  • When: within 30 days of starting.
  • Cost: RM 30 a year in your own name, or RM 60 a year with a trade name.
  • How: on the SSM4U portal, if you are a Malaysian citizen or permanent resident aged 18 or over. You first verify your account at an SSM counter or Tap.it kiosk.

Who has to register

The Registration of Businesses Act 1956 covers every trade, craft, profession or other activity carried on for gain. A job as an employee does not count.

So the test is whether you work for yourself for money. A freelance designer, a wedding photographer, a home tutor, an aircond technician and a makeup artist normally all need to register.

The Act sets no minimum income, and it does not separate full-time from part-time work. A side job you do at weekends for gain normally counts too.

You apply within 30 days of starting. Suppose a makeup artist takes a first paid job on 3 January 2027: the last day to apply is 2 February 2027.

Who does not have to register

The Act leaves out these groups, among others:

  • Employees, for their salary, and charities.
  • A Sdn Bhd, which is registered under the Companies Act instead.
  • A business that consists only of a profession the law limits to qualified, registered people, such as a lawyer.
  • People who go from place to place or house to house selling goods or their handicraft skill, unless they work in or from a building or structure, a motor vehicle with 3 or more wheels or a licensed stall.
  • People with no other business who sell by retail at a weekly fair, for up to 2 days in a row a week, goods grown or made only in Malaysia.
  • Fishermen, poultry and livestock rearers and small farmers working for their own living. There are limits: 25 acres and 5 workers.
  • A craftsman who needs no licence or registration for the craft, works at home, does not display the work for sale in public and has no helpers from outside the household.

If you are close to one of these, ask SSM on 03-7721 4000 before you rely on it.

References: Registration of Businesses Act 1956, sections 1(2), 2, 4 and 5(1), and the Schedule; Federal Territory of Labuan (Extension and Modification of Registration of Businesses Act) Order 1996, P.U.(A) 121/1996.

What happens if you do not register

Running a business that should be registered without registering it is an offence. The maximum is a fine of RM 50,000, 2 years in prison, or both.

There is a more everyday cost. If you should be registered and are not, you normally cannot go to court to enforce a contract made for that business, unless the High Court gives you relief. That makes it harder to act when a customer does not pay.

References: Registration of Businesses Act 1956, sections 8, 12(1)(a) and 12(1)(d).

How to register on SSM4U

Since 24 May 2026 you reach SSM's ezBiz Online through the SSM4U portal.

  1. Create an account. At ssm4u.com.my, choose Register Now, fill in your name, email and MyKad or MyPR number, and confirm the email you receive.
  2. Verify the account. SSM's manual lists 2 ways, both with your card and thumbprint: an SSM counter or a Tap.it kiosk.
  3. Choose a name. Your name as on your MyKad needs no approval. A trade name needs SSM's approval first, and it cannot be too general or look like a registered business.
  4. Fill in the form. Log in, open ezBiz Online, then My Business Services and New Business Registration. Enter your start date, the period, your address, what the business does and its business code.
  5. Pay. Use a credit card or FPX.
  6. Download the certificate. Once approved, it is under Registration Status / History. It stays there for 90 days, so save a copy.

References: SSM, EzBiz Online, SSM4U user manual, and Tatacara Pengguna Ezbiz: Pendaftaran Perniagaan Baharu, version 6.1, 24 May 2026; Business Name Registration Guidelines; SSM notice, 6 May 2026.

Sabah and Sarawak

The Act covers Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan only. In Sabah, the Trades Licensing Ordinance requires a trading licence for most trades, but not professions, issued by the district's administrative officer. In Sarawak, the Business Names Ordinance requires the firms on its list to register within 1 month of starting.

Ask your district office or local council which applies to your work.

References: Trades Licensing Ordinance (Sabah Cap. 144), sections 3 and 4; Business Names Ordinance (Sarawak Cap. 64), section 4.

After you register

  • Put the registration number on letterheads, invoices, bills and other documents that carry your business name. See how to write an invoice.
  • Display the certificate at your main place of business.
  • Tell SSM about changes, such as a new address, within 30 days.
  • Renew before the registration expires: you apply in the 30 days before that date. SSM can offer a compound for late renewal, and carrying on after expiry is an offence. EzBiz takes renewals up to 12 months after expiry.
  • If you stop, tell SSM within 30 days.

In Invois, you enter your SSM number once in your company profile, and it prints on your letterhead. More in Your SSM number in Invois.

Registering does not mean you have met other rules. Income tax with LHDN, SST and any council licence are separate.

This is general information, not legal advice.

References: Registration of Businesses Act 1956, sections 5(8), 5A, 5B, 5D, 11A and 12(1)(b); Registration of Businesses Rules 1957, rule 13(2); SSM, Tatacara Pengguna Ezbiz: Pembaharuan Perakuan Pendaftaran Perniagaan, version 5.1, 24 May 2026.

FAQ

Can I send invoices before I register?

Yes. The SSM number is only required once your business is registered, so leave it off until you have one. Never put a number you do not have, and still apply within 30 days of starting.

I have worked for years without registering. What now?

Register now and give your real start date, because a false statement to SSM is an offence. If you are worried about what that date means for you, ask SSM on 03-7721 4000 before you apply.

Should I use my own name or a trade name?

Your own name is cheaper and needs no approval. A trade name costs double and needs SSM's approval, but customers see a business name on your documents instead of your name.

Sole proprietor or Sdn Bhd?

As a sole proprietor, you and the business are the same in law, so its debts are yours. A Sdn Bhd is a separate legal person, with more rules and costs. Many people who bill their own work start as a sole proprietor; an accountant can tell you when a company makes sense (Companies Act 2016, section 20).

Does registering mean I must charge SST or pay more tax?

No. SST depends on your service and your sales, not on SSM: see Do I need to charge SST? Income tax depends on what you earn, so you declare it to LHDN whether or not you are registered.
