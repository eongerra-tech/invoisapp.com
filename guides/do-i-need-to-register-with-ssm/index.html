Do I need to register with SSM?

If you work for yourself in Peninsular Malaysia or Labuan, you normally must register within 30 days, from RM 30 a year. Here is who is exempt and how to apply.

Invois team, EONGERRA TECH

6 min read · Last updated 6 November 2026

In short
  • Who: in Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan, if you work for yourself to earn money, you normally have to register, even for a side job, at any income. Sabah and Sarawak use their own laws.
  • When and cost: within 30 days of starting; RM 30 a year in your own name, RM 60 with a trade name.
  • How: online at ssm4u.com.my, if you are a Malaysian citizen or PR aged 18 or over. Verify your account once at an SSM counter or Tap.it kiosk, then apply in ezBiz and pay by credit card or FPX.
  • If you do not register: it is an offence, and you normally cannot take a customer who does not pay to court.

Who has to register

The law (Registration of Businesses Act 1956) covers any work you do for yourself to earn money: a trade, a craft, a profession or anything else. A job as an employee does not count.

This normally includes freelancers of every kind, from designers and photographers to tutors and aircond technicians, full time or part time, at any income.

You apply within 30 days of starting. Suppose a makeup artist takes a first paid job on 3 January 2027: the last day to apply is 2 February 2027.

Who does not have to register

The Act leaves out these groups, among others:

  • Employees, for their salary, and charities.
  • A Sdn Bhd, which is registered under the Companies Act instead.
  • Professions that by law only qualified, registered people may practise, such as lawyers, when that is the whole business.
  • Some hawkers who move from place to place, weekly-market sellers of goods grown or made in Malaysia, and small farmers and fishermen, within limits set in the Act.
  • A craftsman who works at home with no outside helpers, needs no licence and does not display the work for sale in public.

If you are close to one of these, ask SSM on 03-7721 4000 or through Live Chat on its website before you rely on it.

The Act covers Peninsular Malaysia and Labuan only. Ask your district office or local council which rule applies to you:

  • Sabah: most trades, but not professions, need a trading licence from the district office.
  • Sarawak: the businesses on the Business Names Ordinance's list register within 1 month of starting.

What happens if you do not register

  • Running a business that should be registered without registering it is an offence: a fine of up to RM 50,000, up to 2 years in prison, or both.
  • You also normally cannot go to court to enforce a contract made for that business unless the High Court allows it. That makes it harder to act when a customer does not pay.

What it costs

You pay for 1 to 5 years at once: a trade name for 3 years costs RM 180.00.

SSM's free scheme SPPP covers 2 years of registration (once only) for Malaysian B40 entrepreneurs and full-time students with no active business at SSM. It runs until its funds are used up, so check that it is still open.

People aged 60 and over have their own free scheme for 1 year (SPPWE).

How to register on SSM4U

Since 24 May 2026 you reach SSM's ezBiz Online through the SSM4U portal. Have ready:

  • your MyKad and an email address
  • your business address and the date you started
  • a short description of your work
  • a credit card or online banking (FPX)
  1. Create an account. At ssm4u.com.my, choose Register Now, fill in your name, email and MyKad or MyPR number, and confirm the email you receive.
  2. Verify the account once. Go to an SSM counter or a Tap.it kiosk with your MyKad or MyPR; your thumbprint is scanned to confirm it is you.
  3. Choose a name. Your name as on your MyKad needs no approval. A trade name needs SSM's approval first, and it cannot be too general or look like a registered business.
  4. Fill in the form. Log in and open ezBiz Online > My Business Services > New Business Registration. Enter your start date, how many years to register for (1 to 5), your address and what you do.
  5. Pick a business code. Choose Add Business Code, search a word for your work and pick at least 1 code.
  6. Pay by credit card or FPX. SSM aims to decide within 1 working day.
  7. Download the certificate under Registration Status / History within 90 days, and save a copy. The registration number on it is the number for your invoices.

After you register

  • Put the registration number on letterheads, invoices, bills and other documents that carry your business name. See how to write an invoice.
  • Display the certificate at your main place of business.
  • Tell SSM about changes, such as a new address, within 30 days.
  • Renew in the 30 days before the registration expires. Carrying on after expiry is an offence; ezBiz still takes late renewals for 12 months, and SSM may offer a compound.
  • If you stop, tell SSM within 30 days.
  • Registering does not cover other rules: a council licence, if your work needs one, is separate.

FAQ

Can I send invoices before I register?

Yes. Leave the SSM number off until you have one, never put a number you do not have, and still apply within 30 days of starting.

I have worked for years without registering. What now?

Register now and give your real start date, because a false statement to SSM is an offence. If you are worried about what that date means for you, ask SSM on 03-7721 4000 or through Live Chat before you apply.

Should I use my own name or a trade name?

Your own name is cheaper; with a trade name, customers see a business name on your documents instead of yours.

Sole proprietor or Sdn Bhd?

As a sole proprietor, you and the business are the same in law, so its debts are yours. A Sdn Bhd is a separate legal person with more rules and costs; an accountant can tell you when a company makes sense.

Does registering mean I must charge SST or pay more tax?

No. SST depends on your service and your sales, not on SSM: see Do I need to charge SST? Income tax depends on what you earn, so you declare it to LHDN either way; for deductions, see business expenses on Form B.
